# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The Secret / Trust Arbitrage:** The business identifies an authentic structural friction in the Dakar consumer electronics market: the primary barrier to purchasing is not product scarcity, but endemic counterfeit risk, lack of dependable local warranty, and slow fulfillment. Coupling certified genuine accessories with a 12-month local replacement warranty and sub-2-hour delivery targets a defensible trust premium rather than competing strictly on bottom-of-the-market pricing.
- **Timing:** Timing is aligned with macro infrastructure readiness. Mobile money penetration in Dakar exceeds 80%, providing established, low-friction digital payment rails (Wave, Orange Money) that eliminate traditional payment friction and enable instant checkout workflows.
- **Geographic Focus for Initial Density:** Limiting initial rapid dispatch to specific, concentrated central Dakar neighborhoods (Point E, Plateau, Almadies, Mermoz, Yoff, Ouakam, Sacré-Cœur) creates an opportunity to establish operational density and brand presence in an affluent urban zone before attempting regional distribution.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Zero Proprietary Moat Against Rapid Copycats (Month 6 Vulnerability):** The business relies on an incremental operational stack—standard imported hardware, lightweight custom PHP/MySQL software, and motorcycle couriers. There is no proprietary technology, no 10x engineering breakthrough, and no exclusive supplier lock-in. An established merchant from Sandaga or a regional e-commerce player with deeper balance sheets can replicate the warranty and courier promise within weeks, sparking price competition that destroys operating margins.
2. **Strategic Bifurcation (Two Subscale Businesses Instead of One Monopoly):** Launching simultaneously as a direct retail operator (with inventory buffer requirements, working capital risks, and return liabilities) and a B2B SaaS marketplace (charging 15,000–35,000 XOF/month plus 4% commission) fragments organizational focus. Network effects cannot develop when neither side of the marketplace has liquidity, leaving both direct sales and merchant tools vulnerable to focused competitors.
3. **Linear Logistics Scaling and Cash-on-Delivery Unit Economic Drain:** Courier fulfillment across Dakar traffic scales linearly rather than generating compounding returns to scale. When combined with Cash on Delivery, transit cancellations, phantom orders, and courier cash-handling leakages directly erode the target 35%–50% gross margins and deplete cash reserves.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. What single product category or micro-demographic in Dakar can SenTechPLUS achieve 80%+ dominance in before expanding across the entire electronics catalog?
2. What stops a well-financed merchant or established e-commerce competitor from copying your 12-month replacement warranty and 2-hour delivery service within three to six months?
3. Do you have exclusive distribution or warranty agreements with any hardware manufacturers, or can competitors buy the identical inventory from the same supply channels?
4. What is the verified customer acquisition cost (CAC) for the first 100 retail customers, and what percentage of orders are fully pre-paid versus Cash on Delivery?
5. Why build a multi-tenant merchant SaaS platform before establishing market liquidity and consumer brand supremacy under your own flag?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Eliminate the SaaS Marketplace to Dominate One Core Beachhead:** Cease the B2B SaaS storefront initiative immediately. Direct all capital and execution bandwidth toward capturing a decisive market share in direct retail for one focused beachhead category (e.g., certified charging accessories or verified audio) within the core central Dakar districts until customer retention and local brand authority are proven.
2. **Secure Exclusive Supply and Warranty Rights:** Establish exclusive local distribution or authorized service agreements with at least two reputable hardware brands, creating a legal and commercial barrier to entry that copycats cannot bypass.
3. **De-Risk Cash on Delivery:** Transition customer payment workflows to prioritize upfront Wave and Orange Money transactions (e.g., through delivery discounts or mandatory digital delivery deposits), capping Cash on Delivery exposure to protect operating cash flow and eliminate courier settlement defaults.
