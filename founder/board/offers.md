# Offers lens
Score: 7/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Favorable market dynamics**: The target market satisfies the four critical requirements of the Offers lens:
  - *Massive pain*: The Dakar electronics market is plagued by counterfeit accessories, high defect rates, and zero buyer recourse from informal merchants.
  - *Purchasing power*: Targeted urban professionals, tech workers, and creators are willing and able to pay an estimated Average Order Value (AOV) of 32,000 XOF (~$52 USD) for guaranteed reliability.
  - *Easy reachability*: Concentrated within well-defined Dakar districts (Plateau, Almadies, Point E, Mermoz, Yoff, Ouakam, Sacré-Cœur) accessible through WhatsApp, TikTok, Instagram, and local search.
  - *Market growth*: Rapid expansion of mobile money adoption (>80% penetration) and consumer tech demand across urban Senegal.
- **Superior value equation for consumers**:
  - *Near-zero time delay*: Under 2-hour motorcycle courier fulfillment in central Dakar turns delayed online shopping into immediate gratification.
  - *Minimal effort and sacrifice*: 1-Click WhatsApp instant checkout and native Wave/Orange Money integration remove account creation hurdles, cart abandonment friction, and banking barriers.
  - *Elevated perceived likelihood of success*: Certified genuine hardware coupled with a 12-month local replacement warranty directly neutralizes the consumer's primary fear of purchasing fake or disposable electronics.
- **Margin protection without price competition**: Rather than engaging in a price race to the bottom against informal open-air markets, the offer targets healthy 35%–50% gross margins backed by speed, authenticity, and risk reversal.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Unhedged warranty liabilities draining operating cash**: A 12-month direct replacement guarantee on imported consumer electronics in Dakar's coastal, humid, and dust-heavy environment poses existential cash-flow risk. If replacement units are funded from buffer stock without formal manufacturer defect credits (RMA), a normal hardware failure rate will rapidly consume the 35%–50% gross margin.
2. **Offer dilution and merchant channel conflict**: Operating simultaneously as a direct-to-consumer retailer and a B2B SaaS storefront (charging 15,000–35,000 XOF/month plus 4% take rate) confuses the value proposition. Physical Dakar merchants will view SenTechPLUS as an existential competitor using their catalog and sales data to sell identical categories with proprietary 2-hour courier advantages.
3. **Cash on Delivery (CoD) refusal and courier drag**: In dense Dakar traffic, CoD transactions carry high cancellation and refusal rates when couriers encounter delays or customer unavailability. Every failed run wastes dedicated courier capacity, locks up inventory in transit, and inflates customer acquisition costs.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. What specific contractual agreements or supplier credit terms back the 12-month replacement warranty, and what technical diagnostics prevent honoring user-induced damage or electrical surge failures?
2. Why would an independent Dakar electronics merchant pay 15,000 to 35,000 XOF monthly plus a 4% commission to a platform that directly retails the same product categories with preferential 2-hour fulfillment?
3. What is the measured refusal and return-to-origin rate for Cash on Delivery orders, and what is the difference in unit contribution between CoD and upfront mobile money (Wave/Orange Money) orders?
4. What mechanisms of real scarcity or urgency exist in the current offer (e.g., restricted weekly batch imports, exclusive brand distributorships, or limited daily 2-hour dispatch windows)?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Drop the B2B SaaS marketplace distraction**: Shelve or spin off the merchant storefront tier to focus 100% of operational focus and capital on perfecting the direct B2C consumer offer stack.
2. **Bound and protect the 12-month replacement guarantee**: Formulate explicit warranty terms (e.g., manufacturer defects only, verified serial tracking, direct supplier reimbursement arrangements) to prevent replacement costs from eroding working capital.
3. **Shift volume from Cash on Delivery to mobile money**: Introduce an exclusive bonus (e.g., free premium braided charging cable or priority dispatch queue) for pre-paid Wave and Orange Money checkouts to drive prepaid adoption above 75% and minimize courier refusal rates.
4. **Introduce genuine scarcity and high-perceived-value bonuses**: Structure inventory releases around verified batch arrivals and bundle low-cost, high-utility add-ons (such as protective cases, cable organizers, or pre-tested QC certificates) instead of discounting core hardware prices.
